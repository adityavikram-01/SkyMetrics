# Final synthetic scenario: scope and evidence

This dataset exists to exercise SkyMetrics' traveller and government workflows before licensed, repeated real fare observations are available. **Every fare, scheduled flight number, collection outcome, remaining-seat state, and forecast implied by these rows is simulated.** The data cannot establish live availability, actual airline prices, or prediction accuracy.

## Unit and coverage

One collection result means one attempted 09:00 IST search of an assumed one-way, economy, nonstop flight for one future departure date. It is not a passenger booking. The fixed product is one adult, INR total payable amount with no baggage/refund/fare-family differences. There are 24 directional routes across the existing 10 cities, 192 assumed daily services across 6E/AI/IX/QP/SG, and morning/daytime/evening/night options on each route. Each service has a stable synthetic flight ID; each departure is searched at 45 through 1 days before travel. The 730 daily collection runs end on 5 October 2026. Expected collection results: `730 × 192 × 45 = 6,307,200` (a failed search remains a result but never becomes an available fare).

The 12 bidirectional route pairs and their *relative service allocations* follow the order of a published [OAG September 2026 domestic scheduled-capacity snapshot](https://www.oag.com/indian-aviation-data). The exact 192 flights, carrier/route pairings, flight numbers, local departure times, seat counts, and continuation of that schedule over two years are **illustrative assumptions**, not copied timetables. The five carriers are selected to demonstrate different pricing behaviours; the source does not prove that all five served every included route.

## Behaviour in the model

Prices share a fixed service identity, route and seasonality profile, weekday/weekend and limited payday effects, flight-time factor, carrier-specific price-hold and volatility choices, booking-horizon pressure, inventory buckets, and correlated route/carrier shocks. A route can enter a multi-day high-demand, promotion, or disruption regime with a rise, plateau, and recovery. Promotions belong to an assumed operating carrier and induce a smaller response from assumed competitors. Directional holiday demand is stronger into Patna before Chhath and out after it; Goa Christmas travel is similarly directional. Selected dates come from public holiday calendars; **the affected routes, windows and uplift sizes are scenario parameters, not measured fare responses**.

The older user-supplied `Freshly_cleaned.csv` was used only as a bounded *relative* route-level check. After filtering to economy, nonstop options on covered directional routes, 17,973 rows across 14 routes remained. A 35% log-relative correction, capped at 0.80–1.25, adjusts those route baselines; its old absolute prices are not carried forward. The file is a transformed 2022-era option table without repeated collection timestamps, so it cannot calibrate present-day fares, booking lead-time movement, individual airline policies, or index accuracy. Its provenance and reuse terms are not independently verified, and the raw CSV is not copied into this repository.

At each search horizon the unrounded target is approximately `route baseline × carrier factor × departure-time factor × flight-specific factor × calendar demand × inventory bucket × lead-time curve × market regime × exp(correlated noise)`. The calendar term depends on route personality, month, travel weekday, selected event windows and limited payday effects. Inventory evolves one day at a time from simulated bookings and cancellations. A carrier-specific hold decision may retain yesterday's quote; otherwise the bounded target is rounded to ₹50. Route-wide multi-day regimes and shared route/carrier shocks correlate flights, while flight-level noise keeps individual histories distinct. These factors are explanatory model controls, **not parameter estimates from current airline data**.

The generator uses deterministic random streams for reproducibility. A held quote, a higher/lower repricing, unavailable inventory, and simulated source failure are distinct states. Any `SOLD_OUT`, `NO_OFFER`, `TIMEOUT`, `RATE_LIMITED`, `PARSE_ERROR`, or `SOURCE_BLOCKED` result has no available price. Internal remaining inventory is latent model state; the product must not label it as airline-reported seats. Prices are constrained and rounded in ₹50 steps. Current event/seasonal coverage is selective, not a comprehensive India holiday model.

## What this supports in SkyMetrics

For travellers, the rows can power route/date fare search, four departure-time choices, nearby-date comparisons, same-flight history, comparable route/carrier/lead-time summaries, and prototype book-or-wait and price-outlook interfaces. Account features such as saved trips and alerts require the application database and notification jobs; the synthetic fare generator alone does not provide them. It cannot provide a real airline booking hand-off or live fare guarantee.

For government analysts, repeated daily observations can power a fixed-basket **SkyMetrics research airfare index**, index history, route/city and directional comparisons, booking-horizon analysis, carrier/time-band analysis, selected-event scenarios, coverage/outcome monitoring, and dated exports. It is **not official CPI or measured airfare inflation**. A valid policy interpretation requires licensed real quotes, stable fare-product definitions, actual schedules and passenger/spend weights, source health evidence, and out-of-sample validation.

## Research and validation boundary

The public [OAG route-capacity page](https://www.oag.com/indian-aviation-data) informs only route prominence. Selected event dates are drawn from the [NHAI 2024 calendar](https://nhai.gov.in/assets/pdf/NHAI_Table_Calendar_2024.pdf), [West Bengal 2025 holidays](https://www.egiyebangla.gov.in/pdf/Holiday-2025.pdf), [central 2025 holiday list](https://www.ignfa.gov.in/document/holidays-list-2025.pdf), and [CAG 2026 holiday list](https://cag.gov.in/defence/new-delhi/en/page-defence-new-delhi-holidaylist). A [Ministry of Civil Aviation statement](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1843408&lang=2&reg=48) describes Indian fare buckets and cheaper advance purchases; an [IIM Ahmedabad study of five routes](https://www.iima.ac.in/sites/default/files/rnpfiles/9959095172015-01-02.pdf) reports lead-time, weekday and competitive effects. The [Yale Cowles airfare paper](https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2022-09/d2103-u3.pdf) also supports dynamic-pricing mechanisms. None of these sources calibrates this scenario's numerical parameters or proves its accuracy for 2026. No restricted booking site was scraped and no live fare feed was obtained.

`audit_final.py` checks sampled route/time/carrier coverage, price movements, holds, and non-identical histories. `verify_generated.py` checks the complete generated database's counts, outcomes, fares, synthetic flags, date range, and referential integrity without loading all rows into memory. Passing these checks establishes structural validity and plausible variety, **not realism or predictive quality**.

## Replacing the old local dataset

Keep the old imported dataset until the new one has passed both audits, been imported into the local SkyMetrics database, and traveller and government API responses work using the new dataset ID. Then update the frontend dataset ID/date, preserve user accounts and alerts, and only remove old observations through a verified database migration or backup-backed cleanup. Do not upload the multi-gigabyte output, raw reference CSV, `.env` files, or local database snapshots to Git.
